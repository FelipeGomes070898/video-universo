## SCENE 5 — chemistry  (What the ocean is made of)

**SCENE NUMBER:** 5
**TIMECODE:** 00:02:17:04 – 00:03:26:11  (2:17–3:26)
**DURATION:** 69.3 s
**NARRATION:** Two instruments did the tasting. One was a mass spectrometer, which weighs the molecules in the gas. The other was a dust analyzer, which examines the ice grains one by one. More than ninety percent of the gas was water vapor. The rest included carbon dioxide, methane, and simple organic molecules, in amounts higher than scientists expected. The ice grains carried salt, and also tiny grains of silica, the stuff of sand. Silica like that forms where hot water, above about ninety degrees Celsius, meets rock. So the ocean is not only there. Its floor is warm, with hot water flowing through the rock. In October 2015, Cassini made its deepest pass through the plume, skimming just forty-nine kilometers above the ice, and its instruments registered molecular hydrogen, roughly one percent of the gas. On Earth, hydrogen like that comes from hot water reacting with rock on the seafloor, and whole ecosystems around hydrothermal vents live off that chemical energy.

## SCENE 11 — dive  (Twenty-two dives)

**SCENE NUMBER:** 11
**TIMECODE:** 00:06:32:13 – 00:07:25:09  (6:32–7:25)
**DURATION:** 52.8 s
**NARRATION:** The final chapter began with a goodbye to Titan, on the twenty-second of April, 2017. That last flyby used Titan's gravity to tilt Cassini's orbit so that it threaded the gap between Saturn and its innermost ring. Nobody had ever sent a spacecraft through that gap. It is roughly two thousand kilometers across, and dust there could damage the spacecraft, so the dish antenna pointed ahead like a shield. Over the next five months Cassini passed through the gap twenty-two times, measuring Saturn's gravity and magnetic field from closer than ever before. On the last five passes it brushed the outermost layer of the atmosphere and sampled Saturn's air directly, for the first time.
